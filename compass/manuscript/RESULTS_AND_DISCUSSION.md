# Age-associated metabolic differences and COQ8A-linked metabolic variation in human MuSCs

## Results

### Older MuSCs show lower predicted CoQ synthesis and selected central carbon reaction scores

We first compared donor-level muscle stem cell (MuSC) metabolic profiles in the original Barthel Index analysis roster, comprising seven young and six older individuals. Raw nuclear RNA counts were aggregated within each donor and converted to counts per million before COMPASS analysis. Cell annotations were held fixed, and donors were treated as the biological replicates. Older donors had lower scores for the terminal CoQ biosynthetic reaction COQ3m_pos, whose annotated product is ubiquinone-10 (two-sided exact rank-label permutation p = 0.01399; BH q = 0.02965 across 6,533 variable reactions; Fig. 1). The 14 reactions assigned to ubiquinone synthesis had the same donor rankings and inferential results (Fig. S2). These coupled model outputs describe a coordinated pathway pattern rather than 14 independent pieces of evidence.

Lower scores in older donors also involved ATP-citrate lyase (ACLY; p = 0.00816, q = 0.02735), acetyl-CoA synthetase (ACS; p = 0.02214, q = 0.03891), citrate synthase (p = 0.00816, q = 0.02735), mitochondrial IDH2 (p = 0.00466, q = 0.02735), and succinate dehydrogenase (SDH; p = 0.00466, q = 0.02735). The pattern was not uniform across the TCA cycle: aconitase and IDH3 were not significantly different. These unadjusted score differences involve CoQ biosynthesis and selected acetyl-CoA supply and TCA-associated reactions. Their interpretation is qualified by the input-size sensitivity analyses below.

### COQ8A expression covaries with predicted metabolic reaction scores among older donors

We next examined whether donor-level COQ8A expression tracked metabolic reaction scores in an expanded cohort of 21 individuals, retaining all eligible donors and stratifying the analysis by age (seven young and 14 older; Fig. 2). Among older donors, COQ8A expression was positively associated with CoQ synthesis (Spearman coefficient = 0.708, permutation p = 0.00604), PDH (0.766, p = 0.00194), ACLY (0.704, p = 0.00650), and SDH (0.677, p = 0.00964). Corresponding associations in young donors were weak and not statistically significant (coefficients 0.036-0.250; p = 0.595-0.963). These estimates describe between-donor variation within each age group.

As a complementary categorical analysis, donors were divided above versus at or below the median COQ8A CPM within each age group. Older high-expression donors had higher CoQ, PDH, ACLY and SDH scores than older low-expression donors (nominal p = 0.02622, 0.01107, 0.02622 and 0.03788, respectively; Fig. S1). None of the 16 age-stratified high-low comparisons passed BH q < 0.05 (minimum q = 0.13986). No high-low difference was detected in the young group. Continuous and categorical results were obtained from the same donors and are complementary summaries, not independent replications.

### Sensitivity analyses delimit the age-specific interpretation

A within-donor negative-control experiment subsequently tested four reactions (CoQ, ACLY, PDH and SDH) using 79 expression profiles. All 13 donors were rerun with their complete input; all four scores exactly reproduced the prior output. For the nine donors with at least ten eligible nuclei, five nuclei were randomly sampled without replacement in three repetitions. Separately, raw pseudobulk UMI counts from each of the 13 donors were binomially thinned with probability 0.1 in three repetitions. Every profile was renormalized to CPM before COMPASS. All four donor-mean scores decreased in 9/9 five-nucleus controls and 13/13 UMI-thinning controls. In young donors, five-nucleus sampling alone reduced mean CoQ score by 0.278, comparable in magnitude to the unadjusted older-minus-young mean difference of -0.295. Resamples were summarized within donor rather than treated as additional biological replicates. These controls demonstrate that input reduction can reproduce a decrease of the magnitude observed between age groups; they do not identify the exact technical contribution to that difference or rule out a biological age effect. The unadjusted age contrast therefore does not currently establish age-specific metabolic decline.

A subsequent audit of the fixed BI13 age comparison identified substantial sampling imbalance: the median retained nucleus count was 65 in young donors and 5 in older donors; corresponding donor UMI totals were 123,011 and 10,361, and detected RECON2 gene counts were 833 and 177.5. Direct reaggregation from raw integer counts reproduced all deposited CPM profiles, confirming total-count normalization. Nevertheless, CoQ scores correlated strongly with donor UMI totals (Spearman coefficient 0.989) and model-gene coverage (0.984). In exploratory HC3 OLS models retaining all 13 donors, the age coefficient remained negative but was not nominally significant after adjustment for coverage (p = 0.231), log1p total UMI (p = 0.427), or cohort plus coverage (p = 0.344). Consequently, the unadjusted age differences cannot currently be separated confidently from sampling and coverage effects; the pathway-wide decline is not established as an age-specific biological effect.

Pearson correlations using untransformed and log1p-transformed COQ8A CPM, covariate-adjusted rank correlations, and age-by-expression interaction models are reported in full (Fig. S3). Across all 21 donors, the CoQ Spearman coefficient decreased from 0.643 unadjusted to 0.403 after adjustment for age group and cohort (approximate p = 0.087), and to 0.230 after additional adjustment for model-gene coverage (p = 0.358). The raw-CPM CoQ age-by-expression interaction adjusted for cohort was not significant (HC3 p = 0.528). Therefore, the significant association in older donors and nonsignificant association in young donors do not establish an age difference in the underlying effect.

## Discussion

The donor-level analysis identified unadjusted age differences and COQ8A-associated variation in metabolic reaction scores. Within-donor input-reduction controls also demonstrated substantial sensitivity to sampling depth. Biological and sampling contributions to the age contrast therefore remain unresolved. Greater COQ8A dependence in older MuSCs remains a hypothesis for independent testing; the age-by-expression interaction was not significant. Coverage adjustment and downsampling are complementary sensitivity analyses, and neither constitutes a definitive correction for all technical and biological differences.

The ACLY association provides a candidate connection to chromatin regulation because ACLY supplies acetyl-CoA for histone acetylation [3]. PDH supports mitochondrial acetyl-CoA production, which can connect indirectly to cytosolic citrate and ACLY-dependent acetyl-CoA supply. SDH links succinate and fumarate metabolism to a distinct potential route involving alpha-ketoglutarate-dependent DNA and histone demethylation [4]. These biochemical connections motivate measurement of acetyl-CoA availability, histone acetylation and demethylation-related states after COQ8A perturbation. The current RNA-derived reaction scores do not establish metabolite-pool changes, chromatin opening, causal direction, or mediation through CoQ.

All focal reactions and statistical presentations were selected in an exploratory research program that had already examined multiple expression thresholds, donor subsets and matching rules. Spearman provides a rank-based summary without assuming linearity; it was chosen for the principal presentation after the exploratory results were available. Pearson results and unsuccessful sensitivity settings are retained rather than treating the principal presentation as a prospectively specified or independently validated test. The continuous donor association also does not replace the separate, less consistent within-donor nucleus high-low analyses. No PAX7/CALCR detection filter was imposed on the principal results.
